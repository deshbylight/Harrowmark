# Case 1: The Drowned Man of Pier 5

Source of truth for `DA_Case01` (`UCaseDefinition`); machine-readable twin: `data/case01.json`, checked by
`tools/case_check.py`. Rules of film, cards, threads and dawn: `docs/GDD.md` section 7. Times are 24-hour
clock. Night 1 is Friday 11 to Saturday 12 November 1949; night 3 ends at dawn on Monday 14 November.

**The twist.** None of the four suspects killed Morrow. The detective did, and he knows it. The voice-over
never lies; it only leaves things out (the Ackroyd narrator, section 11). Everything the player needs is
on film, and some of it is on film before the game begins. Most players will hang Varga. A player who
turns the camera on the back-bar mirror can put the detective's own face on the board and pin it.

## 1. The truth

Captain Kordes of the SS Saint Agatha is running contraband cigarettes and Scotch as "machine parts".
Elias Morrow, customs inspector, has been sniffing at the ship all week. On Thursday Kordes paid the
HarrowMark agency $300 in fifties in a Hotel Meridian envelope (A30) to find something on the inspector.
The detective found it that same night: Morrow at the door of room 9 of the Meridian, a woman's gloved
hand on his sleeve (A29).

On Friday at 19:00 Morrow saw the real cargo in the Agatha's hold. At 20:10 he telephoned the harbour
master for a hold search. Then the detective telephoned him and set a meeting: the end of Pier 5, 23:30,
come alone, about a photograph. Morrow wrote it down as "Meet H.M. 23:30, P5." H.M. is HarrowMark. Every
player reads it as the harbour master.

At 22:40 the detective drove down from the agency office uptown with his camera on its tripod, and at
22:55 he left the car in the dark yard behind the Lighthouse, where he had parked on insurance jobs
before. He did not go into the bar. At 23:05 he crossed the dark east end of the street, let himself
through the harbour-works wicket behind Warehouse 5 with the agency's key and went along the catwalk under
the pier, where neither the crane cab nor the gate booth can see. At 23:15 he reached up from the deck and unscrewed the edge-lamp bulb, so the crane's floods
would show a dark pier end and nothing on it. He waited. Morrow walked out at 23:30; Holt in the cab saw
him go and saw nobody after him, because the man he was meeting was already there.

Morrow did not buy. He said the report went in on Monday and the agency's name would go in with it.
The detective fired one plate into his face: a customs man at a midnight meeting with a private eye was a
second print worth having. Morrow threw up his hand against the flash and came for the camera. The
detective swung the tripod. The first swing bit into the lamp post (A24) and splintered the leg; the
second opened the back of Morrow's head. Morrow went into the water at 23:41. The tripod's brass foot went
with him.

The detective climbed down to the lower landing to see whether Morrow came up, and stood in the wash to
his shins (D06). He did not come up. Back along the catwalk, through the wicket to the car, and at 00:10 he
was in the office uptown sawing the splintered leg square (A28). The splinter in his right palm he left for later.

At 01:40 he was in the darkroom printing the Meridian job when the phone rang. It was Kordes: a customs
man is dead under my ship, the police are holding her as a witness ship until the inquest sits on Monday
at noon, and her charter needs the Monday morning tide. Kordes needs a name in front of the police before
the tide, and he does not much care whose. Fifty a day, two hundred if it is over before he sails. The
detective said yes before he heard the fee. He slid the holder he had fired at the pier back into the
box with its dark slide reversed, and drove back down to the rope. The counter on the camera reads 11.

**Varga, the decoy.** Leo Varga, Morrow's partner, also takes Kordes's money. At 16:30 he lost CL-2's
boathook over the side at Pier 5; it floated under the deck and wedged in the pilings, and he signed the
store book for it like a good officer (A26). At 23:28 he signed the clearance stamp out of the customs
office, walked it to the harbour master's office and countersigned the waived hold search for Kordes
(A22); the stamp was back at 23:52. At 23:53 he took CL-2 out on "patrol" to be seen on the water. Coming
in at 00:25 he slipped off the pontoon making her fast (S19), and at 00:30 he ordered a double at the
Lighthouse, wet to the belt. His lie ("from half past eleven I was out on CL-2") hides a bribe, not a
murder. He knows how a CL-2 hook in the pilings under a dead man looks, so on night 2, with Brannock moved
to the gate booth, he fishes for it with a lantern and a pike pole and fails. On night 3 Kordes pays him
off in the shadow under the gangway.

**The client.** Kordes paid three people that week: the agency on Thursday, Varga and the harbour master,
R. Ashby, on Friday night. He lied about where he was because he was ashore buying his clearance.

## 4. Evidence

### 4.1 Shots (one plate and 10 minutes each, 12 plates a night)

A shot is a framed subject. Taking it gives its anchor cards at once (contact print on the board). A shot
with a detail gives a Detail card when its developed print is examined with the loupe in the darkroom:
at dawn, or mid-night after developing the plates at the office (a 10-minute drive each way, then 30 minutes). A
detail lives as long as its negative: a print developed earlier can go under the loupe again at the
office at any time, free. A print is sharp when the subject fills at least 35% of the frame height (GDD
9.1), and the viewfinder brackets say so before the shot (white sharp, grey grainy); a grainy print
still gives its anchors but no detail, and that one subject may be shot again. The forced wound shot and
the pre-exposed frame 1 are always sharp.

**Frame 1.** Night 1 starts with 11 plates: the first holder in the box was fired at the pier before the
game began (section 1). It cannot be shot; it develops with the others, mid-night or at dawn, whichever
comes first, and its print (A31) lands on the board like any other. Every player gets it.

### 4.5 Theory slots

Pinning a slot makes assertions the red thread can test:

| Slot | Asserts |
|---|---|
| WHO = X | PROP(killer.height = X's height); AT(X, Pier5Edge, 23:35-23:41) only once a time source (A02 or A10) is on the board |
| WITH WHAT = A06 boathook | PROP(wound.shape=torn), PROP(weapon.length=long), AT(Boathook, Pier5Edge, 23:40-23:41) |
| WITH WHAT = A07 bottle | PROP(wound.shape=glass_cut), PROP(weapon.length=short) |
| WITH WHAT = A08 bale hook | PROP(wound.shape=torn), PROP(weapon.length=short) |
| WITH WHAT = A28 tripod | PROP(wound.shape=torn), PROP(weapon.length=long), AT(Tripod, Pier5Edge, 23:40-23:41) |
| WHY | No assertions; checked by the dawn voice-over and the ending |

Without the watch or the crane log the board does not know when Morrow died, so no alibi can break a WHO
pin. The player who skips the time sources can only rule people out by height.

The report sheet makes the rule visible: under the slots a typed line reads "Time of death: ____". It
fills in with 23:41 the moment the player holds A02 or A10; from then on WHO is checked against alibis.

WHO takes a face on the board: Nora, Holt, Kordes and Varga from the start, the detective once A27 is
held (no talk needed). Brannock and the dead man cannot be pinned. The detective's slot card is the
mirror print itself. Nothing breaks him: he has no alibi on the board, he is tall, and the tripod fits
every fact the boathook fits.

The red herrings resolve into sins, not innocence: the lies of Nora, Holt, Kordes and Varga each break
open when proven, and what comes out is a debt, a theft and two bribes. Nobody on the board hides a
murder. The man who does is holding the camera.

Each innocent falls to a different kind of proof: Nora to height (A09), Holt to time (A10, A21), Kordes
and Varga to place (A11, A22, A26). The boathook falls to the paperwork customs keeps on lost gear.

**Why Varga is the trap.** He lies, he is tall, his launch's hook is under the pier, he came in wet, he
fishes for the hook on night 2 and takes an envelope on night 3. Every one of those is true and none of
them is murder. The two cards that clear him (A22 from the cabin, A26 from the store book) cost a gate
and a trip to the customs office, and the player who already has a confession-shaped lie on the wall
rarely makes the trip.

## 8. Endings

"Proven" = WHO = detective, WITH WHAT = A28, WHY in {A29, A30}, and D05 or D06 held. The report goes to
the client with a carbon to Brannock; if A15 is held, the player chooses whether the crates go into the
carbon.

| Id | Condition | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| E1 Whole truth | Proven, A15 attached | The detective hands Brannock the carbon and his own wrists. The Agatha misses her tide; Kordes goes down the gangway in cuffs a few minutes behind him. No fee |
| E2 Confession | Proven, A15 withheld | The detective hands Brannock the carbon and his wrists. The Agatha sails on the Monday tide; Kordes never calls. The Meridian envelope stays in the drawer |
| E3 Not on film | WHO = detective, not proven | Brannock reads it and hands it back: "You want to hang yourself, detective, bring me more than a mirror." The inquest records misadventure. The tripod stands a little crooked by the darkroom door |
| E5 Misadventure | WHO empty when the report goes | The Agatha sails on the Monday tide. The inquest records misadventure; Kordes pays fifty a day and doesn't call again |
| E4 The wrong man | WHO is anyone else | The accused is taken away. Kordes pays in full: "I needed a name before the tide. I didn't care whose." The detective counts the fifties at the bar, and looks up into the mirror |

Rows are checked in this order (E5 before E4, so an empty WHO is never "anyone else").

The case must be closed by the end of night 3 (the tide); otherwise the report goes with whatever is
pinned, and with WHO empty that is E5.

**Reconstruction (Must).** Before the ending text, the player's own prints of the murder play as a
slideshow in the order of the night: the lamp, frame 1, the gouge, the wound, the watch, the tripod. A
beat the player never shot is an empty frame marked "not on film". Frame 1 is always there, because every
player developed it. The last frame is the back-bar mirror: the player's print if they took it, otherwise
the live view over the detective's shoulder at the bar. Every player leaves knowing who did it; only some
of them wrote it down. This is what makes the twist land for the majority who hang Varga, so it is not
cuttable.

Rating on the case card: A (proven, closed by night 2, at most 1 wrong red thread), B (proven),
C (E3), D (E4 or E5). The E1/E2 choice does not change the rating.

## 11. Design notes from the reference books

Techniques taken from the public-domain detective canon (`Docs/Guides/DetectivePublicDomainBooks.md` in
JamLibrary, distilled into atoms), and why each one is here:

| Technique | Where in this case | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The narrator who omits (Ackroyd) | Every VO line; the detective is the killer and never says so | The twist is fair because nothing he says is false. Writing rule: no line may deny, claim ignorance or describe the night from 22:40 to 00:10 |
| The clue the reader already holds | Frame 1 (A31, D05) develops for every player | The proof arrives without being looked for; the question is whether the player asks whose camera it was |
| The ambiguous initials | "Meet H.M." in Morrow's notebook | Reads as the harbour master; A22 shows Ashby at his desk signing at 23:30, so H.M. was somebody else |
| A stopped watch, but corroborated | A02, backed by the crane log's "splash 23:41" (A10) | One independent source makes it fair |
| The negative clue (the dog that did nothing) | S04: nobody walked the lit deck after Morrow; S02: his car in the yard, and he never came in | Points to the hidden route and to the detective at the harbour without naming either |
| The return to the scene, with a reason | A23: Varga fishes for the hook once Brannock has left the rope | Here it is a false lead: a guilty-looking act by a man guilty of something else |
| The missing trace, the listener's doubt | S03: "Rain doesn't come up from below"; answered by S19 | The witness voices the oddity; the answer is mundane |
| One lie per suspect, each hiding a smaller sin | S17, S16, S06, S08 | Red herrings resolve into a debt, a theft, two bribes; none hides murder |
| Each innocent cleared by a different argument | Nora by height, Holt by time, Kordes and Varga by place | Three tools learned, no repetition |
| The tell | Lie lines get one small acting tell | Fair to the attentive player; never confirmed by the UI. The detective has no lines, so no tell to give |
| The client as suspect, with a fee | Kordes: fifty a day, two hundred if it is over before he sails | He wants a name, any name; he already paid the man he hired |
| Recurring names | "J.K." on three envelopes, Hotel Meridian, "C.L. 2" | A name seen twice is a thread the player pulls on their own |
| The detective's own tool | The tripod; the camera that fired frame 1 | The weapon is the object the player walks past every dawn |
| The reconstruction | Ending slideshow of the player's own prints, ending on the mirror | The canon's summing-up, told by the player's evidence; it delivers the twist to those who missed it |

**Known soft spot.** A careful killer would have fogged frame 1. The prologue shows why he didn't: the
phone rang while the holder was in his hand. The VO never explains it.
